Two Quick Fixes for Your Google Ads

Getting your logo back into your ad, and turning on tracking
so you can finally see which ads bring in real jobs.
About 10 minutes. No tech experience needed.

Before you start — the 30-second version

You noticed your logo stopped showing next to your ad. We found the cause, and it's a one-line fix inside your Google Ads account — nothing is wrong with your website.

While you're in there, there's a second thing worth doing that takes five more minutes: turning on conversion tracking, so Google can tell you which ads are actually producing quote requests instead of just clicks.

You'll do three things, in order:

  • 1. Fix the web address in your ad — this brings your logo back.
  • 2. Create a conversion action so quote requests get counted.
  • 3. Send us two short codes, and we'll finish the setup on the website side.
Only Step 3 needs anything from us. Steps 1 and 2 you can do on your own right now. Step 3 is just copying a piece of text and emailing it over.
You don't have to do this alone. We're happy to be on a quick call while you go through it, or to screen-share if a menu doesn't look like it does here.

1Fix the web address in your ad

This is the one that brings your logo back. It's a single field.

Why the logo disappeared: Google stores one logo per exact web address. Your ad currently points at www.410oddjobs.com, but your website's real address is 410oddjobs.com — without the www. Google treats those as two different places, and it only has your logo on file for the one without the www. That's exactly why your logo shows fine in the normal search results but not in your ad.

  1. Go to ads.google.com and sign in.
  2. In the left menu click Campaigns, then click into your junk removal campaign.
  3. In the left menu click Ads (on some accounts this sits under Ads & assets).
  4. Hover over your ad and click the ✏️ Edit pencil that appears, then choose Edit ad.
  5. At the top you'll see a box labelled Final URL. It currently reads something like this:
https://www.410oddjobs.com
  1. Delete everything in that box and paste in this instead:
https://410oddjobs.com/quote/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc
  1. Click Save.
  2. Repeat for every ad in the account. If you have more than one ad running, each one has its own Final URL box and each needs the same change.
That address does two jobs at once. Dropping the www is what fixes the logo. The /quote/ part sends people to a new page we built specifically for your ads — the quote form is the very first thing they see, instead of landing on the homepage and having to scroll down to find it. The bit after the question mark just tells us the visitor came from Google.
The logo won't appear instantly. Google refreshes this on its own schedule — usually a few days, occasionally up to two weeks. There is nothing further to do once the address is saved; it will come back on its own.

2Turn on conversion tracking

Right now Google can tell you how many people clicked. It cannot tell you how many of those clicks turned into an actual quote request. This fixes that.

Without this, every ad looks equally good because all you can see is clicks. With it, you'll be able to see which keywords produce real leads and which ones just burn money — which is the difference between guessing at your budget and knowing.

  1. Still in Google Ads, click Goals in the left menu, then Conversions, then Summary.
  2. Click the blue + New conversion action button.
  3. Choose Website.
  4. It asks for your website. Type 410oddjobs.com and click Scan. Let it run — it takes up to a minute.
  5. When it finishes, look for + Add a conversion action manually and click it. Don't pick any of the actions it suggests on its own.
  6. Fill the form in exactly like this:
    • Goal and action optimization: Submit lead form
    • Conversion name: Quote Form Submission
    • Value: Don't use a value for this conversion action
    • Count: One
  7. Click Done, then Save and continue.
On "Value": if you know roughly what an average job is worth to you, you can enter that number instead and Google will optimise toward higher-value work. If you're not sure, leave it off — it can be changed later and it doesn't affect anything else.

3Send us two short codes

The final piece has to go on the website, which is our side. We just need two values from the screen you're on.

  1. On the conversions list, click the one you just made — Quote Form Submission.
  2. Click Tag setup.
  3. Choose Install the tag yourself (sometimes shown as Use Google tag).
  4. You'll see a box of code. Somewhere inside it is a line that looks like this:
gtag('event', 'conversion', {
  'send_to': 'AW-123456789/AbC-dEfGhIjKlMnOp'
});
  1. Take a screenshot of that whole box and email it to us. That's genuinely the easiest way — you don't need to work out which part is which.

If you'd rather type them out: the part before the slash — highlighted in yellow above, starting with AW- — is the Conversion ID. The part after the slash, highlighted in green, is the Conversion Label. We need both. Your real ones will be different from the example.
What happens next: we add those two codes to your website's thank-you page. From then on, every time somebody submits a quote request, it gets counted in your Google Ads account against the exact keyword that brought them in. Nothing changes for your customers — it's invisible to them.

One optional extra

Step 1 is the real fix for your logo. There is also a second, separate place in Google Ads where you can upload your logo directly, as a belt-and-braces backup — it's called a business logo asset.

It isn't available on every account. Google requires the account to have completed advertiser verification and to have been spending on Search recently. The menu location for it also moves around depending on your account version, so rather than send you hunting for a button that might not be there, let us know if you'd like to add it and we'll find it with you on a quick call. It's a nice-to-have, not a must.
